Embedded2025

DuskTimer

Calendar-Based Night Lighting Controller

Street and building lighting that switches at the right moment every day of the year without a light sensor. A DS1307 real-time clock feeds date and time to an ATmega168 over I²C, and the firmware computes the day length for a fixed latitude and longitude (41.72° N, 44.72° E) to find dawn, sunrise, sunset and the end of evening twilight before driving the output relay. Three buttons set the clock and per-end minute offsets, the LCD colon blinks on the DS1307's 1 Hz output, and a coin cell keeps time through a power cut.

VoxClock

Talking Clock for Blind & Partially-Sighted Users

A clock that answers "what time is it?" out loud, so checking the time never means depending on someone else. Time, date and alarms are delivered entirely by recorded speech in Georgian, through a button interface designed to be used without sight. Built on an ATmega324 with its own PCB, and carried from algorithm and flowchart through Proteus simulation to a working physical unit.

Robotics2024

HexaArm

6-Axis Robotic Manipulator Arm

Six-axis manipulator driven by six servo channels from a single ATmega168: each ADC channel reads a potentiometer and maps it to a servo's PWM pulse width, so every joint can be positioned independently. Three axes place the wrist, the rest orient it — the same arrangement industrial arms use to reach like a human forearm. Full schematic capture, bill of materials and Proteus simulation.

Instrumentation2023

PhaseGuard

3-Phase Voltage & Phase-Angle Meter with Auto-Sequencing

Measures all three line voltages and the phase-shift angles between them, then puts the phases back into correct A-B-C order through relays. Each voltage is sampled twenty times for an RMS figure, and Timer1 clocks the interval between zero crossings to 1 µs before converting it into degrees. A protective interlock keeps the output relay open until the sequence is right. ATmega168, LCD readout, and two boards of my own layout — attenuator and control.

Robotics2023

PathFinder

Ultrasonic Obstacle-Avoiding Car

The second take on the self-driving car, this time with a microcontroller doing the thinking. An ultrasonic range-finder is timed on Timer1 overflow to measure distance, a servo sweeps the sensor to compare the routes ahead, and the car commits to the clearest one. Firmware written in C for AVR, verified in Proteus, and laid out as a PCB.

Analog & Sensors2022

ReflexCar

Two-Motor Car with Infrared Collision Avoidance

A car that steers itself around obstacles with no microcontroller and no remote — the decisions are made in hardware. Infrared reflective sensors feed XOR gates and a 4052 analogue multiplexer, which switch transistor H-bridges to drive the two motors forward, back or into a turn. A study in how far pure logic can go before firmware is needed.

Analog & Sensors2022

WallTrace

In-Wall Cable & Live Wiring Detector

Finds 220 V / 50 Hz wiring hidden behind plaster without touching it, so a break can be located without knocking the wall down. A field-effect input stage picks up the mains field and a 741 op-amp amplifies it into three sensitivity ranges, indicated on LEDs and a 1 kHz buzzer. Entirely discrete — no microcontroller — with the circuit calculated by hand and modelled in Proteus.
